## Why the “Missing Target Technology” warnings matter

The model has many demand-conversion technologies such as:

```text
DSL -> DEMAGRDSL -> AGRDSL
LPG -> DEMCOMLPG -> COMLPG
BIO -> DEMINDBIO -> INDBIO
DSL -> DEMTRADSL -> TRADSL
```

The sector/fuel commodity on the right is produced, but most have no consumer
and no specified demand. Because OSeMOSYS permits surplus disposal under its
energy-balance inequality, these branches can simply remain at zero. That is
why they appear as “Missing Target Technology” in the viewer and why the
historical solve reports zero activity for all 25 end-use demand
technologies.

The warnings are therefore exposing missing model closure, not merely a
cosmetic viewer problem. They also include resources and water-balance
commodities that should not receive final demand. Each warning must be
classified.

The 32 commodities reviewed were:

```text
AGRDSL COMBIO COMDSL COMKER COMLPG COMNGS GEO HYD INDBIO INDCOA INDDSL
INDELCFJIXX02 INDHFO INDKER INDLPG INDNGS PUBWATFJI RESBIO
RESELCFJIXX02 RESKER RESLPG RESNGS SOL TRADSL TRAELCFJIXX02 TRAGSL
TRAHFO TRAKER TRANGS WND WTREVTFJI WTRGRCFJI
```

Classification:

| Class | Commodities | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| End-use energy carriers | 25 sector/fuel commodities | Connect to explicit service demands or a clearly labelled historical accounting sink; do not leave as zero-output stubs |
| Renewable resource carriers | `GEO`, `HYD`, `SOL`, `WND` | Model availability/resource potential; do not add final demand |
| Public water service | `PUBWATFJI` | Add observed service demand and correct surface/groundwater production topology |
| Physical water flux/balance | `WTREVTFJI`, `WTRGRCFJI` | Close the hydrological balance with physically meaningful sink/stock/extraction links; do not add arbitrary demand |

There is also a hidden commercial-electricity problem. `COMELCFJIXX02` is
not flagged as missing because `DEMPUBGWTFJI` consumes it. That does not make
the topology correct: the so-called public-groundwater technology currently
consumes commercial electricity, produces public water, and consumes no
groundwater or recharge commodity.

## Target model design

The final purpose is not to make every historical fuel flow an eternal
constraint. It is to create a model that can answer:

> Which renewable-energy pathways meet a stated target while satisfying
> service demand, respecting the historical fleet and resource conditions,
> and remaining robust to uncertain costs, fuel prices, demand, hydro
> conditions, and technology performance?

For this question:

- exogenous useful-service demand is allowed (`J` historically, `S` in the
  future);
- existing equipment and plant stock is allowed (`J`);
- observed weather, inflow, outages, and fuel prices are allowed (`J`);
- generation, fuel allocation, technology activity, emissions, water
  extraction, and future investments should normally be endogenous (`E`);
- a historical fuel-by-sector accounting case may be forced (`H`) but must
  be a separate diagnostic, never the main calibration claim.

#### Phase 1B — Correct public water

The current topology is wrong:

```text
COMELCFJIXX02 -> DEMPUBGWTFJI -> PUBWATFJI
WTRSURFJI     -> DEMPUBSURFJI -> PUBWATFJI
```

`DEMPUBGWTFJI` has electricity intensity `0.0173` but no groundwater input.

Required design:

1. Decide, from the upstream structure and water evidence, whether
   `WTRGRCFJI` is an annual recharge flow, an extractable groundwater
   resource, or an intermediate used to replenish a separate groundwater
   stock.
2. If it is recharge, introduce a clearly named groundwater availability or
   abstraction commodity/technology rather than pretending recharge itself
   is delivered water.
3. Make public groundwater production consume both raw groundwater and the
   appropriate grid-electricity commodity.
4. Make public surface-water production consume raw surface water and any
   evidenced treatment/pumping electricity.
5. Put observed `PUBWATFJI` service in `SpecifiedAnnualDemand` for
   2020–2024.
6. Keep source mix, pumping energy, and associated emissions endogenous when
   the model has enough information to choose them.
7. Preserve the commercial-electricity series as commercial service
   evidence; do not let the water bug consume it.

Do not guess the groundwater commodity. Record the structural decision,
source, units, and balance equation first.

#### Phase 1C — Replace unclosed end-use outputs with useful services

Preferred service commodities:

| Sector | Candidate service | Candidate competing technologies |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture | Mechanical work or agricultural activity | Diesel machinery, electric machinery, sustainable biofuel option |
| Residential | Cooking/thermal service and separately electric appliance service | Biomass, kerosene, LPG, electric cooking |
| Commercial | Cooking/low-temperature heat and electric service | LPG, kerosene, biomass, electricity |
| Industry | Low-temperature heat, high-temperature heat, motor/electric service | Biomass/bagasse, diesel, HFO, LPG, electricity, future clean heat |
| Road transport | Passenger-km and tonne-km | Gasoline, diesel, EV, biofuel |
| Domestic aviation | Passenger-km or calibrated activity proxy | Jet fuel and future eligible alternatives |
| Domestic navigation | Tonne-km/passenger-km or calibrated activity proxy | Diesel/HFO and future eligible alternatives |

The existing sector/fuel commodities may be retained as intermediate final
energy carriers. Add end-use conversion technologies from those carriers to
services with documented efficiencies. Put demand on the services, not on
each carrier, so future scenarios can switch fuels.

Where no service data exist yet:

- use a clearly labelled provisional accounting layer;
- derive service demand from observed fuel use times an evidenced efficiency;
- retain an `UNALLOCATED` or residual category when sector allocation is
  uncertain;
- attach broad uncertainty bounds;
- do not create fake zeros.

#### Phase 1D — Resource and hydrological closure

- `GEO`, `HYD`, `SOL`, and `WND`: classify as renewable resources with
  availability or potential constraints.
- `WTREVTFJI`: treat as evapotranspiration/evaporation sink tied to weather,
  land, and water balance.
- `WTRGRCFJI`: treat as groundwater recharge or a documented intermediate,
  not a final service.
- Add only the minimum resolution supported by retained data.
- Run mass/energy unit tests for every new chain.

#### Phase 1E — Historical technology availability

For each future option, record its earliest Fiji availability. Retain
credible future options but prevent investment or activity before they were
commercially available in Fiji. Do not remove a future technology merely
because it was absent in 2020–2024.

**Acceptance gate:**

- every topology warning is classified and documented;
- no service demand is met by a nonsensical cross-sector link;
- every active historical service has a complete supply path;
- every resource/sink has a physical role;
- zero-demand stubs are quarantined or connected, not silently discarded;
- the uncalibrated repaired model solves;
- raw and v2.0.x baselines remain reproducible.

Suggested release: `v2.1.0-structural`.

### Phase 2 — Forced historical accounting diagnostic (`H`)

**Objective:** Prove that the repaired structure and statistical boundaries
can represent the observed 2020–2024 accounts before asking the optimizer to
reproduce them independently.

Create a separate run/package, for example:

```text
Fiji_v2_2_H_accounting
```

In this diagnostic only:

- impose reconciled annual final-energy consumption by sector/fuel;
- impose or reconstruct public-water delivery;
- impose documented generation/fuel flows where needed to test balances;
- reconcile grid generation, own use, losses, sales, IPP purchases, and
  off-grid exclusions;
- test electricity, fuel, emissions, water, crop, and land balance closure;
- report every forced variable as `H`;
- derive candidate efficiencies or residual allocations only within declared
  source and engineering bounds.

This is a debugging/accounting model. It may be valuable, but it is not
evidence of endogenous reproduction.

**Acceptance gate:**

- all annual accounting residuals are reported;
- unit conversions and statistical boundaries close within declared
  tolerances;
- no forced quantity is labelled `E`;
- unexplained discrepancies are retained as explicit residuals rather than
  hidden in adjusted data;
- candidate calibration parameters and their bounds are registered.

Suggested release: `v2.2.0-accounting`.

### Phase 3 — Endogenous electricity and public-water calibration

**Objective:** Calibrate only 2020–2022 conditions and parameters, then test
generation mix, fuel use, public-water source mix, pumping, and emissions as
model results.

Historical `J` inputs may include:

- annual service/grid-supply requirement;
- inherited plant and pumping stock;
- documented commissioning/retirement;
- observed fuel prices;
- observed rainfall, inflow, drought, or outage information;
- network losses if represented and observed;
- policies that were actually in force.

Candidate `E` outcomes:

- hydro, thermal, biomass/IPP, wind, and solar generation;
- thermal fuel consumption and heat-rate consistency;
- renewable share;
- public surface/groundwater source mix;
- pumping electricity;
- related emissions.

Use an outer calibration loop. OSeMOSYS optimizes system cost; it does not
estimate calibration parameters by itself.

For each candidate parameter vector:

1. write a separate generated-input case;
2. solve it;
3. extract only 2020–2022 outcomes for the calibration loss;
4. compute transparent per-year and aggregate errors;
5. reject parameter vectors outside registered evidence/engineering bounds;
6. retain the run record and parameter vector;
7. freeze the selected parameters before any 2023–2024 score is viewed.

Use derivative-free search, a designed grid, or Latin-hypercube sampling
rather than assuming smooth derivatives. Keep the loss multi-component and
report the components; do not conceal a poor physical fit behind a single
weighted score.

Initial tolerances:

| Outcome | Calibration/validation target |
|---|---|
| Total grid supply | Within ±5%; normally `J`, so report but do not score as `E` |
| Public-water service | Within ±5%; normally `J` |
| Major generation categories | Within ±10% when denominator is material |
| Sector/fuel consumption | Within ±10–15%, widened where source conflict warrants |
| Renewable share | Within ±5 percentage points |
| Tiny wind/solar rows | Use absolute GWh error, not unstable percentage error |
| Energy and water balances | Close to numerical tolerance after documented losses/sinks |

Do not tune arbitrary costs merely to make dispatch match. Costs, prices,
availability, efficiencies, stocks, and outages must have independent bounds
and provenance.

**Acceptance gate:**

- calibration uses no 2023–2024 outcome;
- parameters are frozen in a dated register;
- every parameter has prior value, selected value, bounds, source/assumption,
  permitted calibration outcomes, and reason;
- scorecard reports all years and forcing classes;
- no positive lower-equals-upper locks on claimed `E` outcomes;
- the model remains structurally and numerically valid.

Suggested release: `v2.3.0-calibration`.

### Phase 4 — End-use service calibration

**Objective:** Reproduce historical carrier allocation as a consequence of
service demand, equipment stocks, prices, and efficiencies, while retaining
future fuel switching.

Priority additional evidence:

- vehicle fleet by type, fuel, and year;
- vehicle kilometres, passenger-km, tonne-km, load factors, and fuel economy;
- household cooking-appliance and fuel ownership;
- commercial kitchens and heat equipment;
- industrial boiler, kiln, motor, and captive-generation stock;
- industrial and commercial LPG sales;
- FRCS import/excise/fuel-release records;
- Fiji Gas and Bluegas sales or market data;
- EFL unrounded sales by customer class;
- Fiji Airways/domestic aviation activity;
- domestic shipping and fishing fuel allocation;
- FSC/bagasse/cane crushing and cogeneration balances.

Historical stock turnover should prevent instant switching that could not
have occurred. New investment may compete only when historically available.
COVID-era transport variation should be represented through service drivers
or observed exogenous conditions, not absorbed into permanent technology
efficiency.

If evidence remains insufficient:

- retain a residual/unallocated fuel bucket;
- use source-specific uncertainty intervals;
- run multiple boundary interpretations;
- reduce the calibration claim rather than manufacture precision.

**Acceptance gate:**

- observed final-energy rows are comparison targets, not all hard demands;
- service balances close;
- stock constraints are historically plausible;
- sector/fuel results are scored annually;
- ambiguity and unallocated quantities are visible;
- future carrier substitution remains possible.

### Phase 5 — Held-out validation, 2023–2024

**Objective:** Test the frozen 2020–2022 calibration on unseen years.

Allowed after freeze:

- insert genuinely exogenous 2023–2024 conditions such as observed service
  demand, rainfall/inflow, outages, fuel prices, and inherited stock;
- solve and score.

Not allowed:

- alter a parameter because the 2023 or 2024 result misses;
- use 2023–2024 generation or sector/fuel outcomes to choose the parameter
  vector;
- silently redefine the source boundary after seeing the error.

If a miss reveals a structural defect, create a new calibration experiment
and a new held-out design. Do not retroactively preserve the old validation
claim.

Report:

- annual observed and modeled values;
- absolute error;
- percentage error only for stable denominators;
- renewable-share error;
- balance closure;
- forcing class;
- evidence coverage;
- materiality;
- worst annual miss;
- separate calibration and held-out aggregate metrics.

**Acceptance gate:**

- frozen-parameter checksum matches the calibration record;
- held-out run is reproducible;
- every comparison has `E/J/H/S` classification;
- grade is assigned only for the declared scope;
- failures remain visible.

Suggested release: `v2.4.0-validation`.

### Phase 6 — Robustness and renewable-target pathways

**Objective:** Use the validated model to identify robust pathways to a
renewable-energy target, not a single supposedly certain forecast.

Create at least:

- reference scenario;
- renewable-target scenario with the exact legal/analytical target
  definition;
- stronger and weaker demand cases;
- high/low fossil-fuel price cases;
- high/low technology cost and financing cases;
- wet/dry hydro sequences;
- high/low resource availability cases;
- delayed/accelerated project cases;
- alternative final-energy boundary/allocation cases;
- reliability or reserve proxies appropriate to the time resolution.

Initial uncertainty ranges where better distributions are unavailable:

- uncertain sector fuel allocations: roughly ±15–30%;
- demand growth: evidence-based low/central/high;
- fuel prices: historical volatility and authoritative outlook cases;
- capital cost, fixed/variable O&M, efficiency, lifetime, and availability:
  technology-specific evidence ranges;
- hydro: historical wet/dry sequences rather than one average;
- grid losses and station use: observed range;
- existing stock and retirement: documented range or discrete alternatives.

Compare pathways on:

- target compliance;
- discounted system cost and annual cash-flow burden;
- capacity and generation additions;
- fuel imports and exposure to price shocks;
- emissions;
- land and water implications that are actually represented and calibrated;
- stranded assets and retirement;
- build-rate feasibility;
- energy not served or adequacy proxy where available;
- decisions that remain common across many plausible futures.

The useful answer is a robust sequence of near-term decisions and branching
points, not one perfect build schedule. Report:

- no-regret actions;
- decisions that depend on a particular assumption;
- trigger conditions for later choices;
- scenarios in which the target becomes infeasible or very costly;
- limitations caused by annual/four-slice resolution.

**Acceptance gate:**

- all future inputs are `S` and sourced or bounded;
- policy target is implemented and independently checked;
- scenario differences are attributable and reproducible;
- pathway conclusions survive a declared set of sensitivities;
- no claim exceeds the calibrated scope;
- official investment or reliability decisions are not based on an annual
  exploratory model alone.

Suggested release: `v2.5.0-policy-candidate`.

## Stop conditions

Pause and report rather than improvising if:

- the transferred active inputs contain OHC entities;
- the checkout is the GitHub `v2.0.0` tag rather than current `main`;
- the rerun changes dispatch or scores unexpectedly;
- retained source hashes differ;
- a historical quantity cannot be assigned a defensible boundary or forcing
  class;
- 2023–2024 outcomes have already influenced a claimed calibration;
- a service topology requires a new commodity whose physical meaning cannot
  be established;
- a policy conclusion changes sign under plausible source-boundary
  interpretations.

## Definition of success

This work succeeds when the model can:

1. reproduce selected 2020–2022 historical outcomes without directly forcing
   those outcomes;
2. reproduce 2023–2024 held-out outcomes within declared, evidence-aware
   tolerances using frozen parameters;
3. keep forced accounting results visibly separate;
4. preserve service competition needed for electrification and renewable
   substitution;
5. trace every observation, assumption, calculation, transformation, solve,
   and release;
6. identify renewable-target decisions that remain sensible across plausible
   futures.

It does not succeed merely because it solves, matches a forced energy balance,
or produces one least-cost future.
