## Historical electricity boundary

The EFL annual report's “Generation Statistics for the Past Ten (10) Years,”
printed page 88, supplies total grid generation for 2020–2024. The FBoS
energy-account PDF identifies its physical supply-and-use workbook in
Appendix 1, page 3. Workbook sheet `Energy SUT`, annual rows 2020–2024,
supplies:

- commercial electricity use;
- industrial electricity use;
- domestic electricity use;
- household own-generation output; and
- electricity distribution loss.

The retained boundary extract performs only these explicit transformations:

```text
central-grid residential
  = domestic total - household own generation

station-use/boundary residual
  = EFL gross grid generation
  - commercial
  - industrial
  - central-grid residential
  - distribution loss
```

The residual is a calculated reconciliation term. It is not presented as a
directly measured station-use quantity.

## LEDS facts used by the projection

The LEDS describes its LEAP-based bottom-up electricity-demand method on
printed pages 46–51. The active calculation uses the following reported
inputs.

| Printed locator | Reported fact used |
|---|---|
| p. 46 and BAU assumptions on p. 51 | 182,282 households in 2013 and 0.38% annual household growth |
| p. 46 | Urban household shares: 52.98% (2013), 55.57% (2020), 57.37% (2025), 58.97% (2030), 60.51% (2035), 62.01% (2040), 63.46% (2045), and 64.93% (2050) |
| pp. 46–48 and p. 51 | Urban/rural refrigerator adoption, urban air-conditioning adoption, and 90% television adoption in grid-connected households |
| Table 4, p. 47 | Annual conventional/efficient appliance intensities: refrigerator 480/440 kWh, lighting 301/243 kWh, air conditioning 1,500/1,300 kWh, and television 240/150 kWh |
| pp. 47–48 | MEPS turnover assumptions used to blend conventional and efficient appliance intensities |
| p. 48 | 500 kWh per grid-connected household for other electrical appliances |
| pp. 49 and 51 | BAU-unconditional commercial electricity growth of 2.6% per year |
| pp. 49 and 51 | Industrial grid-electricity growth of 2.0% per year |
| pp. 50–51 | Sector demand uncertainty of approximately ±10%, retained as a future sensitivity rather than embedded in the base projection |

The active model does **not** import the LEDS 2013 commercial or industrial
electricity quantity. It applies the LEDS growth rates to observed FBoS 2024
sector anchors. Linear interpolation between stated milestone years and the
normalization of the residential stock index to 2024 are modeller
transformations recorded under `C-1C-COM`, `C-1C-IND` and `C-1C-RES`.

## MICS facts used by the projection

MICS Table SR.2.1, printed page 29, reports central-grid electricity access
for 93.9% of urban households and 74.2% of rural households. Phase 1C holds
those two area-specific access rates constant while the LEDS urban/rural
household mix changes.

MICS Tables TC.4.1 and TC.4.2, printed pages 157–158, provide cooking
technology and fuel shares. Those shares are retained for a later
useful-cooking-service gate but do not supply an energy quantity; they
therefore do not affect the active electricity-demand calculation.

## Reproduce the retained projection evidence

The comparison columns use the validated Phase 1C accounting checkpoint,
which preserves the Phase 1B aggregate path while applying the historical
sector split:

```bash
python Fiji_v2_CLEWs_calibration/scripts/apply_fiji_phase1c_bottom_up_demand.py \
  --evidence-only \
  --source-case Fiji_v2 \
  --comparison-case Fiji_v2_Phase1C_Accounting_Test
```

The historical difference from the accounting control must be zero for every
year from 2020 through 2024. The 2050 bottom-up difference must be
`-3.144434659171 PJ` at the retained precision.
